Your life is one line. Everything else is a thread that can come back.

What it is

A picture of where your attention actually is

Most of what tires you out is not the work in front of you — it is everything running in the background. The argument you keep replaying. The letter you have not answered. The comparison that flares up every time you open your phone. One Current gives each of those a line of its own, so instead of a vague weight you see exactly what is open, how strongly it pulls, and how long it has been running.

One main line

Your life, moving through Now. Everything on it is where you actually are.

Threads

Anything that split off and keeps pulling — a person, a fear, a task, a season of life. Each has a loudness, and the line shows it: louder threads run thicker and more restless.

Loudness

Threads left undecided grow louder on their own, day by day — just like real worries. Any honest decision quiets them again.

Integration

Threads are not deleted or suppressed. When one has given you what it can, it curves back into your main line and travels with you.

Living themes

From quiet Riverbed to koi ponds, pomeranian pups, balloons, cats, anglerfish, ghosts, and dragons — each open thread becomes a small creature that settles when you decide.

In your language

The whole app speaks English or Spanish — every word it says changes, while your own words stay exactly as you wrote them.

How it works

The whole loop takes about a minute

One Current is not a place to spend time. It is a place to make one small, honest decision and leave.

1 Name what is pulling at you

Tap + and write it down in your own words. A new thread appears on the timeline immediately — roughly when it started, colored, with the strength of its pull.

That alone changes something: the pull now has a shape and a place, instead of circling in your head.

Every open thread stays visible until you decide what it needs.

2 Say how loud it is

Tap a thread and a quick panel opens: its name and one dial — how loud is this thread right now? Set it and go; that touch is not a commitment to do anything.

Or skip the panel entirely: press the thread and slide your thumb up or down, and its loudness dials right there on the timeline.

Loudness is honest over time too. A thread left undecided grows louder day by day, and the line shows it — thicker, more restless — until you give it a decision.

One control first. Everything else stays folded away.

3 Decide what it needs today

When you want to do more, open the full menu and answer one question: what does this thread need from you now?

Act — take one small step. Integrate — bring back what still matters. Note — add what just happened. Or say honestly: nothing can be done about it right now — and that counts as a decision too.

Four honest answers. No forms, no scoring.

4 The line changes

Your decisions show up on the timeline itself. Steps you chose appear ahead of Now. Threads you decided on today are marked. A thread you integrate curves back into the main line and stays part of your history.

The Actions view gathers it into one glance: which threads still need a decision today, and which are already settled.

Undecided above, decided below — nothing more to track.

5 Go deeper only when you want to

Every thread has a quieter door: understand this thread. There you can name what from it still belongs to you now — confidence, rest, connection, closure — compare where it began with where you are, and notice what feels less available while it stays open.

All of it is optional. Nothing here is required.

Take what is still yours; leave the rest where it happened.

6 See how gathered you are

A small chip in the corner shows your wholeness — how many threads are pulling on you, and how loudly. When you are scattered, it tells you plainly, and points at the one decision that would gather you most right now.

The same loudness flows into your history: day by day you can see which feelings your open threads were holding, and how deciding on them gave the energy back.

Not a score to optimize — a weather report, and one next step.

Your companion

A small animated character lives on your timeline. He patrols your threads, spots what needs attention, and reacts every time you make a decision — like having a patient, encouraging friend keeping tabs on things with you.

Why it works

You were never built to hold it all at once

Nothing is wrong with you. A mind holding six open worries is a mind doing exactly what minds do — rehearsing anything unfinished, over and over, so it isn't lost. One Current gives all of it somewhere to sit. Here is what happens when it does.

The moment it's outside your head, it stops being everything

A worry you're carrying has no edges. Drawn as a line with a beginning and an end, it becomes one thing among a few — finite, and visibly smaller than you.

That's the whole first move. You don't have to solve it. You just have to set it down somewhere you can see it.

The mind rehearses open loops uninvited — but experiments find the intrusions quiet down once the loop is written down concretely, even though nothing has been done about it yet. See the studies

Give it a name and the volume comes down on its own

One Current only ever asks for a few words in your language — not a score, not a category. That plain naming is doing more than record-keeping.

You'll feel it as the difference between a hum you can't place and a sentence you could say out loud.

In brain-imaging work, putting a feeling into words quiets the amygdala — the brain's alarm — while lighting up the region doing the naming. Naming isn't nothing; it's regulation. See the studies

Resting counts. There is no streak to break

Deciding that nothing can be done today is a real answer, and the app records it as one. The thread goes quiet, and you did that on purpose.

No counters, no rising numbers, nothing that punishes you for a hard week. The only thing that ever grows is the part of you that isn't somewhere else.

We built the playful layer from what trials support — game elements help people keep going, not feel better by themselves — and left out what the same literature warns against. See the studies

Nothing gets crossed out. It comes back into you

When a thread is finished for now, it curves back and rejoins your line — and you choose what it hands over. The patience you found. The self-respect. It goes into the line, not into a bin.

And if it returns one day, that isn't failure either. You meet the newer, smaller version of it, standing on everything the last one gave you.

A study of how apps draw broken progress found the drawing itself changes behaviour: breaks shown as repairable keep people going, breaks shown as final make them quit. Nothing here is ever drawn as final. See the studies

The idea underneath

Your own language worked this out a long time ago

Listen to how everyone already talks about their inner life:

get a grip hold that thought put it down let it go carry a worry handle it drop it pick it back up

Every one of those is a hand word. Of all the metaphors available, the one our whole species reached for is: thoughts are objects, and hands handle them.

So we stopped treating that as a figure of speech and built it. A thread you can see, touch, turn down, set aside and pick up again. We call the working idea the Graspability Hypothesis — a feeling becomes easier to steady roughly in proportion to how graspable you can make its shape.

Said plainly: One Current is built on this research, not proven by it. It has not been through a clinical trial, and we will never pretend the app treats anything. The full page shows every study, including the ones that complicate our case.

Privacy

Your threads stay on your device

What you write in One Current is stored locally on your device — not on our servers. Creating an account lets you back up your history to the cloud and share with your psychologist using codes, but those features are opt-in. Your backup is encrypted before it leaves your device. We have no ads, no analytics, and we never sell your data.

Share codes are one-time and expire in fourteen days. Your psychologist sees only the threads you chose to include. You can export your full data as a file, and delete your account and everything in it at any time from Settings.
